Nature, Hills & UNESCO Heritage

Outdoor Activities & Iconic Landmarks

Bangladesh is known for its lush greenery, the Royal Bengal Tiger, the longest natural sea beach in the world, and misty hill tracts. Explore our top-ranked outdoor destinations below.

Photo: Shihabur Rahman · CC BY-SA 4.0 #1 Ranked • UNESCO

Mangrove Sundarbans

Sundarbans is the largest mangrove forest on earth and a UNESCO World Heritage site located between Bangladesh and India, with the majority in Bangladesh. It is home to the last remaining Royal Bengal tigers and diverse wildlife. Visiting this tranquil forest is a once-in-a-lifetime experience.

Khulna / Satkhira Wildlife & Boat Safari
Photo: Masum-al-Hasan Rocky · CC BY-SA 4.0 #2 Ranked • Adventure

Chittagong Hill-Tracts

A naturally breathtaking region full of hills and home to many indigenous communities of Bangladesh. It is the premier destination for multi-day hill trekking and boat rides along the stunning Sangu River during monsoon.

Bandarban / Sangu River Trekking & Culture
Photo: Syed Sajidul Islam · CC BY-SA 4.0 #3 Ranked • Tea Capital

Enjoy the Tea of Sreemangal

Sreemangal is the tea capital of Bangladesh in the north-east. Cycle through peaceful trails inside lush green tea plantations, explore Lawachhara National Park, and trek to the scenic Hamham Waterfalls.

Sylhet Division Cycling & Rainforest
Photo: Shakhawat Hossen Shafat · CC BY-SA 4.0 #4 Ranked • Lake Cruise

Rangamati, Kaptai & Lake

Famous for the expansive Kaptai Lake surrounded by emerald hills. Enjoy a full-day boat ride across the water, shop for fine handmade tribal fabrics at local handicraft markets, and visit the serene Rajban Bihar Buddhist monastery.

Rangamati District Boating & Handicrafts
Photo: Md. Ahsanul Haque Nayem · CC BY-SA 4.0 #5 Ranked • 8th Century

Archeological Paharpur

Home to Somapura Mahavihara, the most impressive archaeological site in Bangladesh and a UNESCO World Heritage site. Built in the 8th century, it was the second-largest Buddhist monastery south of the Himalayas where scholars gathered from across Asia.

Naogaon, Rajshahi Ancient Architecture
Photo: Masum-al-Hasan Rocky · CC BY-SA 4.0 Cloud Paradise

Cloud Valley, Sajek

One of the most popular mountain destinations in Bangladesh, accessible by Chander Gari (Jeep), CNG, or bike. When white clouds drift across the ridge and touch your skin at sunrise, you truly feel like you are living in paradise.

Baghaichhari, Rangamati Cloud Viewing
Photo: Mirza Salman Hossain Beg · CC BY-SA 3.0 Coral Island

Saint Martin's Island

Bangladesh's southernmost island is known for its clear blue water, coral-rock shoreline, and sandy beaches. Enjoy the Bay of Bengal scenery while respecting the island's fragile marine environment.

Teknaf, Cox's Bazar Island & Sea Beach
Photo: Lahsim Niasoh · CC BY-SA 4.0 National Landmark

Bangladesh National Museum

Chronologically organized across departments of ethnography, decorative art, history, natural history, and world civilization. Features a rich conservation laboratory originally curated by Nalini Kanta Bhattasali (1914–1947).

Shahbag, Dhaka History & Ethnography
Photo: Press Information Department · Public domain March 7, 1971

Sheikh Mujib's March 7 Speech

An archival photograph of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman addressing a vast crowd at Ramna Racecourse in Dhaka on March 7, 1971. His historic speech became a defining moment in Bangladesh's independence movement.

Ramna Racecourse, Dhaka Historic Speech · 1971
