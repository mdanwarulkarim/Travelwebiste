6 Fundamental Strategies

Packing Tips to Travel Smart

Packing is pivotal. Forget an essential item and you're left scrambling; pack too much and you end up burdened with heavy bags and pricey airline baggage fees. Master these 6 fundamental rules.

Rule #1

Make A Packing List

When it comes to packing, procrastinators fall short. Start your packing process days or even weeks ahead of your departure date; this gives you time to craft a complete list and purchase any additional items you might need. Creating a packing list is a fail-safe way to never forget something important.

Rule #2

Roll, Don't Fold

Many travel experts—including backpackers who stuff months' worth of clothing into a compact pack—agree that rolling is superior to folding. Tightly rolled clothes take up significantly less space than folded ones and are far less prone to getting deep wrinkles from fold creases.

Rule #3

Never Check Essential Items

Keep your valuable and essential belongings in your carry-on bag, never in checked luggage. Your passport, identification, money, credit cards, jewelry, and electronics should always stay with you onboard in case an airline delays or misplaces your checked bag.

Rule #4

Know Your Airline's Baggage-Fee Policy

Figuring out airlines' tricky baggage-fee policies is key to any budget-minded strategy. While most international flights include at least one checked bag, domestic and budget carriers often charge steep fees for checked or oversized carry-on bags. Always verify weight limits before leaving home.

Rule #5

Follow The 3-1-1 Liquid Rule

Avoid security-line confiscations and delays by following the 3-1-1 rule for carry-on liquids: all liquids, gels, and aerosols must be in 3.4-ounce (100ml) bottles or smaller, placed inside a single, clear, quart-size zip-top bag per passenger.

Rule #6

Use Your Personal Item Wisely

Most airlines permit one carry-on bag plus one personal item (such as a laptop backpack, camera bag, or tote) that fits under the seat in front of you. Maximize this space for in-flight comfort items, chargers, medications, and travel documents.
